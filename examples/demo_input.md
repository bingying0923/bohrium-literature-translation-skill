Abstract

We present a microfluidic platform for rapid detection of lead ions in drinking water. The sensor achieves a limit of detection of 0.8 µg/L within 12 min, which is lower than the WHO guideline of 10 µg/L [1]. Unlike previous colorimetric assays, our method does not require sample pretreatment and remains stable at 4 °C for 30 days (Fig. 1). These results suggest that the platform may be suitable for field deployment in resource-limited settings.

1 Introduction

Heavy metal contamination of drinking water is a persistent public health problem [2]. Conventional laboratory methods such as ICP-MS provide excellent sensitivity, but they require centralized facilities and trained operators. Portable optical sensors have therefore attracted attention, although most reported devices still suffer from limited shelf life and matrix interference.
