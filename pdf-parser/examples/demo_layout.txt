Abstract  We report a low-cost colorimetric sensor for lead quantification   Lead ions are captured on a chelating membrane
The sensing principle relies on a colour change that is read by a phone camera   The colour change is quantified with a calibration curve
Samples are filtered and passed through the membrane before imaging   Recovery is evaluated on spiked tap water
We test the sensor on water samples collected in three cities   The limit of detection is 0.8 µg/L
Each assay takes about twelve minutes to complete   Field deployment is only discussed as future work

1 Introduction
Lead contamination of drinking water remains a public health problem in many regions. Portable sensors may enable screening where laboratory facilities are unavailable.
2 Methods
We collected 36 water samples from three cities between March and June 2024. Samples were filtered and passed through a chelating membrane.
The calibration curve was fitted with a linear model, and the limit of detection was calculated as three times the standard deviation of blank measurements.
