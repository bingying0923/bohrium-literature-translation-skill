Abstract

We investigate whether a low-cost colorimetric sensor can quantify lead ions in drinking water at concentrations relevant to public health. The sensor uses a chelating membrane and a smartphone camera.

[page 2]

1 Introduction

Lead contamination of drinking water remains a persistent public health problem [2]. Unlike previous laboratory-bound methods, portable optical sensors may enable field screening, but their detection limits are often insufficient.

2 Methods

We collected 36 water samples from three cities between March and June 2024. Samples were filtered and passed through a chelating membrane; colour changes were recorded with a smartphone camera and converted to concentration with a calibration curve. The limit of detection was 0.8 µg/L, and each assay took 12 min.

3 Results

The sensor quantified lead ions across the tested range with a limit of detection of 0.8 µg/L, which is lower than values reported previously (Fig. 2). Recovery from spiked samples ranged from 92% to 108%.

4 Discussion

These results may suggest that the method could be applied in resource-limited settings. A limitation of this study is that only three cities were sampled, and field validation in humid conditions has not yet been performed.
